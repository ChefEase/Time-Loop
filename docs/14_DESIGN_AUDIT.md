# Design audit

**Assessment:** the central theft → witness → pursuit → missed delivery chain is a concrete, testable causal puzzle. The murder false victory and the final ordinary minute give it a clear narrative payoff. The source is a strong concept bible, but not yet an executable specification or validated puzzle.

All resolutions below are **PROPOSED v0.2** unless explicitly identified as formatting normalization. None claims author approval or player validation.

## Design tensions to test

**Ethical framing:** stopping a child reporting theft is an interesting unintended consequence, but must not be the sole good solution. Rescuing the parcel, preventing the theft, or protecting the courier should remain meaningful alternatives.

**Redundant optimization:** preventing the entire theft helps relay, coolant, and Mercer at once. That is a satisfying discovery, but risks making later final loops rote. Arthur, information access, and coordinated readiness must add decisions without adding arbitrary chores.

**Emotional credibility:** Simon's tape is evidence, not a universal persuasion code. Arthur's response must respect his grief; Sophie needs agency and a reason to help.

**Information fairness:** players should infer causal changes through observable consequences. The notebook cannot solve the entire graph automatically, and offscreen failures need discoverable evidence.

**Scope:** five conditional NPCs can validate the premise. Twenty-four complete schedules, reactive dialogue, multiple endings, and a remembered-knowledge interface are a substantial solo production. Do not treat the finished roster as an obligation to implement all of it before the prototype proves useful.

## Audit outcome

The proposed paper model can demonstrate separate bottlenecks and a candidate coordinated path. It cannot yet establish full-map feasibility, alternate-ending consistency, all partial interventions, credible persuasion, or player curiosity. Those remain explicit gate work.
