# Game vision

**CANON v0.1**

## Identity

**Title:** 8:15. Possible later subtitle: *A Greywick Mystery*.

**Genre:** time-loop detective / causal simulation / narrative mystery. **Perspective:** 2D top-down. **Setting:** Greywick, 1998. **Window:** 8:00 AM–8:15 AM. **Developer:** solo. **Platform:** Windows / Steam first.

> You have fifteen minutes to understand and manipulate the lives of an entire town before a temporal catastrophe kills everyone and resets the day.

Every morning at exactly 8:15, Greywick is destroyed. At 8:00, Avery wakes again. Twenty-five major characters, including the player, inhabit the same fifteen minutes. People lie, steal, argue, and fall in love. One man is murdered. A machine beneath the town wakes up. Eventually everyone dies. The world resets; the player remembers. There are no levels or stat upgrades. Learning is the only way to become more capable.

## Player transformation

Confused outsider → observer → investigator → detective → manipulator → master of Greywick.

Initially: “Who are these people and what the hell just happened?”

Eventually: “Daniel will steal Mercer's key in fourteen seconds. Ruth will see Theo thirty seconds later. If she chases Daniel, Jonah never reaches the substation. So I need Clara to stop Theo before 8:03.” This is a fantasy illustration, not an authoritative schedule.

## Five pillars

1. **Knowledge is progression.** No XP, levels, or intelligence bonuses.
2. **The world is deterministic.** Identical initial state and player actions produce identical outcomes.
3. **Causes matter more than events.** Knowing why Mercer dies is more useful than knowing when.
4. **Small world, extreme depth.** One town and approximately 25 major characters, with bounded but meaningful interactions.
5. **Player interference creates story.** World behavior + intervention → new state → new consequences.

## North star

> Does this make understanding and manipulating Greywick more interesting?

Players should eventually recognize a bell, a turn, a lie, a theft, a flickering light, and a gunshot as parts of a morning they understand. The payoff is reaching 8:15, waiting for destruction, and discovering that 8:16 is possible.

The victory is not universal happiness. It is that tomorrow is possible.
